# Sources and License Rules

Every fact in the book must trace back to a real source. This page lists the main sources and what we're allowed to do with each.

## License cheat sheet

| Source | License | What we may do |
|---|---|---|
| NIH: NINDS, NIMH, NIA, NIDA, NEI, NIDCD pages | U.S. federal government work. Generally public domain, but some images are licensed from third parties. | Paraphrase and cite freely. Don't copy images unless the page marks them public domain. Always link the page. |
| CDC pages (HEADS UP concussion, stroke signs, sleep, FASD) | U.S. federal government work, generally public domain | Paraphrase and cite freely; link the page. |
| OpenStax *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (2025) | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Adapt with attribution (title, OpenStax, link, license). It's compatible with our CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. |
| Wikipedia | CC BY-SA 4.0 | Use as a reference and a starting point. Paraphrase, don't paste. Verify claims in primary sources. |
| Peer-reviewed papers (PubMed / PMC) | Varies | Cite them. Short quotes only (under 15 words). Never reproduce figures. |
| SfN *Brain Facts* (2018) | © Society for Neuroscience, all rights reserved | **Topic map only.** Never copy text, figures, or question wording. |
| Paid Brain Bee prep (quiz camps, courses) | Commercial | Do not use. |

## Rules for writers

1. **Open every URL before citing it**, and confirm it loads and actually says what you claim.
2. If a statistic can't be verified, leave it out. Don't guess, and don't round a vague memory into a precise-sounding number.
3. For drugs and therapies, state the approval status and year exactly. Investigational means investigational.
4. For each chapter's `references.md`, include 8 to 10 entries, each with: the linked title, the source, and 1 to 2 sentences on why it's useful to a high school reader.
