# Pre-Test and Post-Test: Neurons, Signals, Synapses, and Brain Emergencies

**For the study leader:** give the same 20 questions before and after the sessions. Print **only Part 1** for students. Keep Part 2 (the answer key) separate, and don't show it to students until the study is over. The questions are original and are based on Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 14 of *From Neuron to Bedside*.

## Part 1: Student test sheet

**Anonymous ID number:** ______  **Circle one:** Pre-test  /  Post-test

**Instructions.** This test is not graded and does not affect any grade. Don't write your name. Choose the **one best** answer for each question and circle its letter. If you don't know, make your best guess. You have about 15 minutes.

**1.** What is a nerve?

- A. A single neuron with an extra-long axon
- B. A bundle of many axons wrapped together, like wires in a cable
- C. A type of glial cell found only in the brain
- D. The tiny gap between two neurons

**2.** Which type of neuron carries information from your skin toward your spinal cord and brain?

- A. Motor neuron
- B. Interneuron
- C. Sensory neuron
- D. Schwann cell

**3.** In the brain and spinal cord, which cell makes myelin?

- A. The oligodendrocyte, which can wrap segments of many different axons
- B. The Schwann cell, which wraps one segment of one axon
- C. The astrocyte, which wraps blood vessels
- D. The microglia, which wrap damaged neurons

**4.** Along a myelinated axon, the signal is renewed at small bare gaps between the myelin segments. What are these gaps called?

- A. Axon hillocks
- B. Synaptic clefts
- C. Dendrites
- D. Nodes of Ranvier

**5.** What mainly forms the blood-brain barrier?

- A. A layer of astrocytes covering the outside of the brain
- B. Cells lining the brain's tiny blood vessels, sealed tightly together, with support from astrocytes
- C. The skull and the fluid around the brain
- D. The myelin wrapped around axons

**6.** A neuron rests at about −70 mV. About what voltage must the inside reach (its threshold) to fire an action potential?

- A. −90 mV
- B. +35 mV
- C. −55 mV
- D. −80 mV

**7.** What happens during the rising phase of an action potential?

- A. Potassium ions flow out of the cell through potassium channels.
- B. The sodium-potassium pump moves three sodium ions out of the cell.
- C. Chloride ions rush into the cell.
- D. Voltage-gated sodium channels open and sodium ions rush into the cell.

**8.** One stimulus just barely reaches a neuron's threshold. A second stimulus is three times stronger. Compared with the first, what does a single action potential from the stronger stimulus look like?

- A. The same size, because action potentials are all-or-none
- B. About three times taller
- C. About one-third as tall
- D. It depends on how long the axon is

**9.** Which axon would carry an action potential the fastest?

- A. A thin axon with no myelin
- B. A wide axon wrapped in myelin
- C. A thin axon with no sodium channels
- D. A wide axon with no myelin and no nodes

**10.** How does lidocaine numb a patient's lip at the dentist?

- A. It blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, so the nerve's axons can't reach threshold and pain signals can't travel to the brain.
- B. It permanently destroys the nerve that carries pain.
- C. It blocks acetylcholine receptors on the lip muscles.
- D. It speeds up the sodium-potassium pump so neurons rest more.

**11.** Which list puts the steps at a chemical synapse in the correct order?

- A. Neurotransmitter binds receptors → calcium enters → vesicles fuse → action potential arrives
- B. Vesicles fuse → action potential arrives → neurotransmitter binds receptors → calcium enters
- C. Calcium enters → neurotransmitter binds receptors → action potential arrives → vesicles fuse
- D. Action potential arrives → calcium enters → vesicles fuse and release neurotransmitter → neurotransmitter binds receptors

**12.** Which neurotransmitter is the main excitatory ("go") neurotransmitter in the brain?

- A. Glutamate
- B. GABA
- C. Glycine
- D. Serotonin

**13.** What does a receptor antagonist do?

- A. It binds a receptor and activates it, copying the natural neurotransmitter.
- B. It produces the exact opposite effect of the natural neurotransmitter.
- C. It binds a receptor without activating it, so the natural neurotransmitter can't act there.
- D. It pumps neurotransmitter back into the presynaptic terminal.

**14.** Why does caffeine make a tired person feel more alert?

- A. It gives neurons extra energy to fire.
- B. It activates adenosine receptors.
- C. It boosts the effects of GABA.
- D. It blocks adenosine receptors, so the "time to rest" signal from adenosine can't get through.

**15.** At the neuromuscular junction, how is the acetylcholine signal mainly ended?

- A. Acetylcholine is pumped back into the motor neuron.
- B. An enzyme, acetylcholinesterase, splits acetylcholine in the cleft.
- C. Microglia swallow the acetylcholine.
- D. Acetylcholine stays bound until the muscle is tired.

**16.** In the BE-FAST stroke warning signs, what does the "T" stand for?

- A. Temperature: check for a fever
- B. Tongue: check whether the person can stick out their tongue
- C. Time: call 911 right away and note when symptoms started
- D. Tired: let the person rest and check again in an hour

**17.** What is a hemorrhagic stroke?

- A. A stroke caused by a blood vessel in the brain leaking or bursting
- B. A stroke caused by a clot blocking an artery to the brain
- C. A short blockage that clears on its own in a few minutes
- D. A burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain

**18.** A classmate falls to the floor with a seizure, stiffening and jerking. Which response is correct seizure first aid?

- A. Hold the person down so they stop moving.
- B. Put a spoon or wallet in their mouth so they don't swallow their tongue.
- C. Give them a sip of water as soon as the jerking stops.
- D. Stay with them, time the seizure, move hard objects away, and turn them on their side if they are not awake and aware.

**19.** During a soccer game, a player bumps heads with an opponent. She doesn't black out, but she feels "foggy," has a headache, and forgets a familiar play. What should happen?

- A. She can keep playing, because she never lost consciousness.
- B. She should sit out for 10 minutes and go back in if she feels better.
- C. She should be removed from play right away and stay out until a health care provider clears her.
- D. She should stay in a dark room with no activity for two weeks.

**20.** A patient suddenly develops weakness that is much worse in the **left leg** than in the left arm, and the face is normal. Which blocked artery best explains this?

- A. Left anterior cerebral artery (ACA)
- B. Right anterior cerebral artery (ACA)
- C. Right middle cerebral artery (MCA)
- D. Left posterior cerebral artery (PCA)

## Part 2: Answer key (for the study leader only; do not print for students)

Each item lists the correct letter, a short reason, and the book section where the idea is taught. Use this key for scoring and for checking item-by-item results after the study.

| # | Answer | Why | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | A nerve is a cable made of many axons bundled together. A neuron is one cell. | Ch 1, Sections 1.3 and 1.4 |
| 2 | C | Sensory (afferent) neurons carry information from the body toward the CNS. | Ch 1, Section 1.5 |
| 3 | A | Oligodendrocytes make CNS myelin and can wrap segments of many axons. Schwann cells do this in the PNS, one segment each. | Ch 1, Section 1.7 |
| 4 | D | Nodes of Ranvier are the bare gaps where the signal is renewed. | Ch 1, Section 1.6 |
| 5 | B | The barrier is formed mainly by tightly sealed cells lining the brain's capillaries, with support from astrocyte end-feet. | Ch 1, Section 1.9 |
| 6 | C | A common textbook threshold value is about −55 mV, about 15 mV above rest. | Ch 2, Section 2.5 |
| 7 | D | In the rising phase, voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open and sodium floods in. K⁺ outflow drives the falling phase; the pump works slowly in the background. | Ch 2, Section 2.5 |
| 8 | A | All-or-none: a stronger stimulus does not make a bigger spike. Strength is coded by firing rate. | Ch 2, Sections 2.6 and 2.8 |
| 9 | B | Larger diameter and myelin both raise conduction velocity. | Ch 2, Section 2.9 |
| 10 | A | Lidocaine binds inside voltage-gated sodium channels; with enough blocked, the axon can't reach threshold. The effect wears off. | Ch 2, Section 2.10 |
| 11 | D | Action potential arrives, Ca²⁺ enters, vesicles fuse and release transmitter, transmitter binds receptors. | Ch 3, Section 3.3 |
| 12 | A | Glutamate is the brain's main excitatory neurotransmitter. GABA is the main inhibitory one. | Ch 3, Section 3.7 |
| 13 | C | An antagonist binds without activating, blocking the natural neurotransmitter. It does not create an "opposite" signal. | Ch 3, Section 3.8 |
| 14 | D | Caffeine is an antagonist at adenosine receptors. It hides tiredness rather than adding energy. | Ch 3, Sections 3.1 and 3.8 |
| 15 | B | At the NMJ, acetylcholinesterase splits acetylcholine, so the signal ends by enzyme rather than reuptake. | Ch 3, Sections 3.6 and 3.9 |
| 16 | C | T is Time: call 911 right away and note when symptoms started. | Ch 14, Section 14.7 |
| 17 | A | A hemorrhagic stroke happens when a brain blood vessel leaks or bursts. Option B is ischemic; C describes a TIA. | Ch 14, Section 14.6 |
| 18 | D | Stay, Safe, Side. Never put anything in the mouth or hold the person down; no food or water until fully awake. | Ch 14, Section 14.10 |
| 19 | C | CDC: remove the athlete from play right away and keep them out until a health care provider clears them. Most concussions happen without loss of consciousness. | Ch 14, Sections 14.3 and 14.4 |
| 20 | B | The ACA supplies the midline leg area. Each hemisphere controls the opposite side, so left leg weakness points to the right ACA. | Ch 14, Section 14.8 |
