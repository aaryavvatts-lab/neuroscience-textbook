# Pilot Study Kit: Does Learning With *From Neuron to Bedside* Help?

This folder is a ready-to-run kit for a small, minimal-risk pilot study. It asks one question: after students use a few chapters and MicroSims from *From Neuron to Bedside*, do their scores on a short neuroscience test go up?

This folder is **not** published on the book's website. It's meant for a student researcher, a teacher, or a club advisor who wants to collect honest evidence about whether the book helps students learn.

## Stop: approval comes first

**Do not recruit anyone, hand out any form, or collect any data until your study is approved** by a review committee: your school's Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Scientific Review Committee (SRC), or another IRB. This is required by the science fair rules from Society for Science (ISEF) and by student journals such as the *Journal of Emerging Investigators* (JEI). See `protocol.md`, Section 8, for the details and the source links.

## What's in this folder

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | This overview and the step-by-step checklist |
| `protocol.md` | The research plan: question, design, sample, timeline, data rules, and ethics. Submit it (adapted) to your review committee. |
| `pre-post-test.md` | A 20-question multiple-choice test on Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 14, with the answer key in a separate section. The same test is used before and after. |
| `feedback-survey.md` | An anonymous survey: 8 agree/disagree items and 2 open questions |
| `consent-assent-templates.md` | A parent/guardian permission form and a student assent form, with [bracketed] blanks to fill in |
| `data-template.csv` | A blank spreadsheet with the columns you will need. It holds no names. |
| `analysis-guide.md` | How to compute averages and gains, run a paired t-test or a Wilcoxon signed-rank test in Google Sheets or Python, and report the results honestly |

## Steps

1. **Read everything in this folder.** Decide which sessions you will run and who will lead them.
2. **Find an adult mentor.** A teacher or club advisor should supervise the study. JEI requires an adult senior author on every paper.
3. **Fill in the blanks** in `protocol.md` and `consent-assent-templates.md` (every `[bracket]`).
4. **Submit to your review committee** (IRB or SRC) with the protocol, the test, the survey, and the consent and assent forms. Fill out any forms your science fair or journal requires.
5. **Wait for written approval.** Make changes the committee asks for. Keep a copy of the approval letter or signed form.
6. **Recruit and collect permission.** Hand out the parent permission and student assent forms. Only students with both signed forms (unless your committee says otherwise in writing) take part.
7. **Session 1:** give the anonymous pre-test (about 15 minutes), then start the first chapter and MicroSim.
8. **Sessions 2 to 3 (or 4):** use the chosen chapters and MicroSims. Follow the session plan in `protocol.md`.
9. **Final session:** give the same test again as the post-test, then the feedback survey.
10. **Enter the data** in `data-template.csv`. Store it as described in `protocol.md`.
11. **Analyze** with `analysis-guide.md`. Report what you found, including the limits.
12. **Share the results** with participants, your committee if required, and the book's author through the [GitHub issues page](https://github.com/aaryavvatts-lab/neuroscience-textbook/issues/new/choose). Never share raw data that could identify anyone.

## What this pilot can and can't show

This is a **pilot**: a small first try. It can show whether scores went up after the sessions and how students felt about the book. It **can't** prove that the book caused the gain, because there is no comparison group, the sample is small, and the students chose to join. `analysis-guide.md` explains how to say this clearly in a report.

## A note on conflicts of interest

If the person running the study also wrote or helped write the book, say so in the report and to the review committee. It doesn't make the study invalid, but readers need to know. One way to reduce bias is to have someone else score the tests using the answer key.
