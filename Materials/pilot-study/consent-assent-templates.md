# Parent/Guardian Permission and Student Assent Templates

**How to use these templates:** replace every item in [square brackets] with your study's details, then delete these instructions. Your review committee (IRB or SRC) may require changes or its own forms; use whatever it approves. Do not hand these out until the study is approved. Keep signed forms in a separate envelope from the test papers, and never link them to ID numbers.

For participants who are 18 or older, adapt the parent form into an adult consent form ("you" instead of "your child") and have the participant sign it.

---

## Template 1: Parent/Guardian Permission Form

**Study title:** [Learning neuroscience with an open textbook and interactive simulations: a pilot study]

**Student researcher(s):** [name(s), grade, school]
**Adult supervisor:** [name, role, school, email or phone]
**Approved by:** [name of IRB or SRC], [date of approval]

Dear Parent or Guardian,

Your child is invited to take part in a small research study at [school or club name]. Please read this form, ask any questions, and decide whether you give permission.

**What is the study about?**
We want to find out whether students learn neuroscience when they use a free online textbook, *From Neuron to Bedside*, and its interactive simulations. We also want to know what students think of the book.

**What will my child do?**
During [number] regular [club meetings / class periods] between [start date] and [end date], your child will:

1. take a short 20-question multiple-choice quiz about the brain (about 15 minutes),
2. take part in [2 to 3] sessions of reading and using online simulations, which are normal [club / class] activities,
3. take the same quiz again (about 15 minutes), and
4. fill out a short survey about the book (about 5 minutes).

The total extra time is about 35 minutes. The sessions themselves are activities your child would do in [club / class] anyway.

**Is it anonymous?**
Yes. Your child will not write their name on the quiz or survey. They will use a random ID number that no one links to their name. We will not collect grades, school records, or any personal information. Results will only be reported as group averages.

**Are there risks?**
The risks are minimal, no more than a normal class activity. Your child might feel unsure on a quiz question. One session covers stroke, concussion, and seizures; the leader will give a heads-up first, and your child may skip any question or activity.

**Are there benefits?**
Your child may learn some neuroscience. The results may help improve a free textbook for other students. There is no payment or reward.

**Is it voluntary?**
Yes. Taking part is completely voluntary. Saying no, or stopping at any time, will not affect your child's grades or standing in [the club / class] in any way. Students who do not take part still join the regular activities.

**How will the information be kept safe?**
Paper quizzes and surveys will be stored in a locked place at school by [adult supervisor] and destroyed by [date]. The typed data, which have no names, will be kept in [a password-protected school account]. This signed form is stored separately from the quizzes.

**Questions?**
Contact [adult supervisor name] at [email or phone]. For questions about your child's rights as a research participant, contact [IRB/SRC contact person, role, and email or phone].

**Please sign and return by [date].** You may keep the top part of this form.

## Template 2: Student Assent Form

**Study title:** [Learning neuroscience with an open textbook and interactive simulations: a pilot study]
**Researcher(s):** [name(s)]  **Adult supervisor:** [name]

Hi! We are doing a small study to see whether a free online neuroscience book, *From Neuron to Bedside*, and its simulations help students learn. We would like you to take part.

**What would I do?**

- Take a short 20-question quiz about the brain (about 15 minutes).
- Do [2 to 3] normal [club / class] sessions with the book and simulations.
- Take the same quiz again.
- Fill out a short survey about what you thought of the book (about 5 minutes).

**Will people know my answers?**
No. You won't write your name. You'll use a random ID number, and nobody will keep a list matching your name to your number. We will only report group averages.

**Does the quiz count for a grade?**
No. Your answers will not affect any grade or your place in [the club / class].

**Do I have to do this?**
No. It's your choice. You can say no, stop at any time, or skip any question, and nobody will be upset with you. Even if your parent or guardian said yes, you can still say no.

**What if I have questions?**
Ask [researcher name] or [adult supervisor name] at any time.
