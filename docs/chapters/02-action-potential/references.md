# References: The Action Potential

1. [2.3 Principles of Bioelectricity](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/2-3-principles-of-bioelectricity) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Explains where the resting potential comes from, with a table of ion concentrations and equilibrium potentials. It is a free, college-level next step after this chapter's battery analogy.

2. [2.4 Mechanisms of Neural Signaling](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/2-4-mechanisms-of-neural-signaling) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Walks through the rising phase, falling phase, undershoot, and refractory period, and compares conduction speeds in myelinated and unmyelinated axons. Source for this chapter's 0.5 to 10 m/s and 150 m/s figures and the 28% energy estimate.

3. [12.4 The Action Potential](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-4-the-action-potential) - OpenStax, *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* - A clear step-by-step account with the classic textbook values (rest −70 mV, threshold −55 mV, peak +30 mV), plus all-or-none firing, absolute and relative refractory periods, and saltatory conduction.

4. [35.2 How Neurons Communicate](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/35-2-how-neurons-communicate) - OpenStax, *Biology 2e* - Written for introductory biology students, so it is a gentle review. It gives the ion concentration table used in this chapter and the 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in pump ratio.

5. [A quantitative description of membrane current and its application to conduction and excitation in nerve](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1392413/) - Hodgkin AL, Huxley AF. *The Journal of Physiology*, 1952 (PubMed Central) - The landmark paper that modeled the action potential in the squid giant axon. The math is advanced, but skimming the figures shows how the first quantitative model of a nerve impulse was built.

6. [The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 1963](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1963/summary/) - NobelPrize.org - Official summary of the prize shared by John Eccles, Alan Hodgkin, and Andrew Huxley for discovering the ionic mechanisms of nerve cell excitation and inhibition.

7. [Local Anesthetics: Review of Pharmacological Considerations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3403589/) - Becker DE, Reed KL. *Anesthesia Progress*, 2012 (PubMed Central) - A dental-focused review explaining how local anesthetics act on sodium channels, why pain fibers are blocked before pressure fibers, and why epinephrine is added. Background for this chapter's Bedside case.

8. [FDA Approves Novel Non-Opioid Treatment for Moderate to Severe Acute Pain](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-novel-non-opioid-treatment-moderate-severe-acute-pain) - U.S. Food and Drug Administration, January 30, 2025 - The official announcement of suzetrigine (Journavx), the first approved drug in a new class of non-opioid pain medicines that target sodium channels in peripheral pain pathways.

9. [Tetrodotoxin: The State-of-the-Art Progress in Characterization, Detection, Biosynthesis, and Transport Enrichment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11676112/) - Zhang X, Qiao K, Cui R, Xu M, and colleagues. *Marine Drugs*, 2024 (PubMed Central) - A review of the pufferfish neurotoxin: how it blocks sodium channels, what poisoning looks like, and why treatment is supportive because there is no specific antidote.

10. [Firing Messages: Ion Channels and Action Potentials](https://www.brainfacts.org/brain-anatomy-and-function/cells-and-circuits/2022/ion-channels-and-action-potentials-111422) - BrainFacts.org, Society for Neuroscience - A short, readable explainer on ion channels, resting potential, and threshold, useful for a quick review before a Brain Bee.
