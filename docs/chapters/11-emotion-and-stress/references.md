# References: Emotion, Stress, and the Body

1. [Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd) - National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) - A plain-language guide to PTSD: who develops it, the four symptom groups, how it is diagnosed and treated, and how to help a friend. It is the source for this chapter's symptom table and diagnostic time frame.

2. [PTSD Treatment Basics](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/tx_basics.asp) - U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD - Explains that PTSD treatment works and names the talk therapies and medications with the most evidence. A clear starting point for anyone curious about how PTSD is treated.

3. [Talk Therapy](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/talk_therapy.asp) - U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, National Center for PTSD - Short descriptions of Prolonged Exposure, Cognitive Processing Therapy, EMDR, and other supported therapies, including Written Exposure Therapy. Helps readers see what happens in each kind of treatment.

4. [I'm So Stressed Out! Fact Sheet](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/so-stressed-out-fact-sheet) - National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) - Explains the difference between stress and anxiety, how long-lasting stress can affect the body, and practical coping strategies. Written for teens and young adults.

5. [12.2 Neural Mechanisms and Circuitry of the Stress Response](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/12-2-neural-mechanisms-and-circuitry-of-the-stress-response) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - A free college-level textbook section on the sympathetic response, the HPA axis, negative feedback, and how the amygdala, hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex respond to stress. A good next step for readers who want more depth.

6. [13.3 What Is the Contribution of Brain Structures in Emotional States?](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/13-3-what-is-the-contribution-of-brain-structures-in-emotional-states) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Covers the Papez circuit, Klüver-Bucy syndrome, patient S.M., the amygdala and fear conditioning, and the cingulate and insular cortex. Useful background for the limbic system and amygdala sections.

7. [Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamic%E2%80%93pituitary%E2%80%93adrenal_axis) - Wikipedia - An overview of the HPA axis, including CRH, ACTH, cortisol, negative feedback, and the daily cortisol rhythm. Handy for review, but check key claims against the primary sources above.

8. [The human amygdala and the induction and experience of fear](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3030206/) - Feinstein, Adolphs, Damasio, and Tranel, *Current Biology* (2011) - The study of patient S.M., who lacks both amygdalae and showed almost no fear in real-life tests. A readable example of how rare patients teach us about the brain.

9. [Fear Extinction as a Model for Translational Neuroscience: Ten Years of Progress](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4942586/) - Milad and Quirk, *Annual Review of Psychology* (2012) - A review of how the amygdala and prefrontal cortex control fear extinction in rats and humans, and why that matters for treating anxiety and PTSD. Best for advanced readers.

10. [A Brief Exposure-Based Treatment vs Cognitive Processing Therapy for Posttraumatic Stress Disorder: A Randomized Noninferiority Clinical Trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5843538/) - Sloan, Marx, Lee, and Resick, *JAMA Psychiatry* (2018) - The trial behind this chapter's "What's new in research" box, comparing five sessions of Written Exposure Therapy with 12 sessions of Cognitive Processing Therapy. Shows how clinical trials test whether a shorter treatment works as well.
