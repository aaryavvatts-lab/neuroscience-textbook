# References: The Aging Brain and Neurodegeneration

1. [What Happens to the Brain in Alzheimer's Disease?](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers-causes-and-risk-factors/what-happens-brain-alzheimers-disease) - National Institute on Aging (NIH) - A clear walk through amyloid plaques, tau tangles, inflammation, and brain shrinkage. It's the best single page for picturing what goes wrong inside the Alzheimer's brain.

2. [Memory Problems, Forgetfulness, and Aging](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/memory-loss-and-forgetfulness/memory-problems-forgetfulness-and-aging) - National Institute on Aging (NIH) - Compares normal age-related forgetfulness with warning signs of dementia and lists treatable causes of memory problems. Useful if someone in your family is having memory changes.

3. [How Is Alzheimer's Disease Treated?](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers-treatment/how-alzheimers-disease-treated) - National Institute on Aging (NIH) - Summarizes symptom medicines and the anti-amyloid antibodies lecanemab and donanemab, including ARIA and MRI monitoring. Good for understanding what treatment really offers.

4. [FDA Converts Novel Alzheimer's Disease Treatment to Traditional Approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-converts-novel-alzheimers-disease-treatment-traditional-approval) - U.S. Food and Drug Administration - The FDA's July 2023 announcement for lecanemab, with the trial result and a plain explanation of ARIA and the boxed warning. Shows how a drug moves from accelerated to traditional approval.

5. [FDA approves treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis associated with a mutation in the SOD1 gene](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-associated-mutation-sod1-gene) - U.S. Food and Drug Administration - The FDA's April 2023 notice for tofersen, explaining its antisense mechanism and why the accelerated approval rested on neurofilament light. A real example of a biomarker shaping a drug decision.

6. [Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als) - National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NIH) - Covers symptoms, genetics, diagnosis, and current treatments for ALS in accessible language.

7. [Huntington's Disease](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/huntingtons-disease) - National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NIH) - Explains the CAG repeat, the 50 percent inheritance risk, symptoms, and symptom treatments. Pairs well with this chapter's genetics section.

8. [18.1 Memory is Classified Based on Time Course and Type of Information Stored](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/18-1-memory-is-classified-based-on-time-course-and-type-of-information-stored) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - A free, open textbook section that connects memory systems to Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment. A good bridge between Chapter 9 and this chapter.

9. [Hypothetical model of dynamic biomarkers of the Alzheimer's pathological cascade](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2819840/) - Jack CR Jr and colleagues, *Lancet Neurology*, 2010 (free full text on PubMed Central) - The widely cited paper behind this chapter's MicroSim, proposing that amyloid becomes abnormal years before neurodegeneration and symptoms. Challenging, but the figures are worth studying.

10. [Cognitive reserve in ageing and Alzheimer's disease](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3507991/) - Yaakov Stern, *Lancet Neurology*, 2012 (free full text on PubMed Central) - A review explaining brain reserve and cognitive reserve and why some people with Alzheimer's brain changes stay sharp longer. Recommended for advanced readers.
