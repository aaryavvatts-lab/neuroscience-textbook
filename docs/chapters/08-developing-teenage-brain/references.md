# References: The Developing and Teenage Brain

1. [The Teen Brain: 7 Things to Know](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/the-teen-brain-7-things-to-know) - National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) - A short, reliable fact sheet on how the teen brain changes, including prefrontal maturation, sleep timing, stress, and mental health. A good first read for any student who wants the official science behind the "teen brain" headlines.

2. [About Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders (FASDs)](https://www.cdc.gov/fasd/about/index.html) - Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) - Explains the types of FASDs, their signs, how they are diagnosed, and why there is no known safe amount of alcohol during pregnancy. It is the main public health source for this chapter's Bedside case.

3. [Understanding Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/understanding-fetal-alcohol-spectrum-disorders) - National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA) - Covers how common FASDs are, why they are often missed, and the range of treatments and supports. Useful for seeing how researchers and clinicians think about FASD as a lifelong condition.

4. [Brain Basics: The Life and Death of a Neuron](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-life-and-death-neuron) - National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) - A readable overview of how neurons are born, migrate along radial glia, connect, and die. It pairs well with Sections 8.4 and 8.5.

5. [5.2 Growth and Development of the Early Brain](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/5-2-growth-and-development-of-the-early-brain) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - A free college-level textbook section on neural stem cells, neurogenesis, radial glia, and the inside-out layering of the cortex. Good for readers who want more detail than this chapter gives.

6. [5.4 Experience Dependent Plasticity](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/5-4-experience-dependent-plasticity) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Covers critical periods, Hubel and Wiesel's kitten experiments, fetal alcohol syndrome, and the adolescent brain in one section. Helpful for connecting the "windows" idea to both learning and harm.

7. [Dynamic mapping of human cortical development during childhood through early adulthood](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC419576/) - Gogtay and colleagues, *PNAS* (2004) - The well-known study that followed children with repeated MRI scans and showed sensory areas maturing before higher-order areas like the prefrontal cortex. Its time-lapse brain "movies" are famous and worth a look.

8. [Extraordinary neoteny of synaptic spines in the human prefrontal cortex](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156171/) - Petanjek and colleagues, *PNAS* (2011) - Found that prefrontal synaptic spines are two to three times more dense in childhood than in adulthood and that pruning continues through the twenties. This is one of the main pieces of evidence behind "development continues into the twenties."

9. [A Social Neuroscience Perspective on Adolescent Risk-Taking](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2396566/) - Steinberg, *Developmental Review* (2008) - An influential review explaining why risk-taking rises in adolescence and falls in adulthood, based on a reward system and a control system that mature on different schedules. Readable for motivated high school students.

10. [Synaptic pruning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptic_pruning) - Wikipedia - A general overview of synaptic pruning, its timing, and the cells involved, with links to the original research. Use it as a starting point, then check its claims against the primary sources above.
