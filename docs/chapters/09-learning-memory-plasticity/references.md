# References: Learning, Memory, and Neuroplasticity

1. [The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2000: Press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2000/press-release/) - NobelPrize.org - The official announcement of the prize shared by Arvid Carlsson, Paul Greengard, and Eric Kandel. Its Kandel section explains, in plain language, how sea slug synapses store short-term and long-term memories.

2. [Eric R. Kandel: Nobel Lecture, "The Molecular Biology of Memory Storage: A Dialog between Genes and Synapses"](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2000/kandel/lecture/) - NobelPrize.org - Kandel's own account of the *Aplysia* experiments. It's challenging reading, but motivated students will see how a career-long research program unfolds.

3. [The molecular biology of memory: cAMP, PKA, CRE, CREB-1, CREB-2, and CPEB](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3514210/) - Kandel, *Molecular Brain* (2012), PubMed Central - A free review of how habituation and sensitization change the sensory-to-motor synapse, and how long-term memory needs new proteins and new synapses. It's the source for the chapter's synapse-counting results.

4. [The Legacy of Patient H.M. for Neuroscience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2649674/) - Squire, *Neuron* (2009), PubMed Central - A clear, readable history of what five decades of testing H.M. taught scientists about memory systems, including the mirror-drawing experiment. It's the best single article on the case.

5. [Loss of Recent Memory After Bilateral Hippocampal Lesions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC497229/) - Scoville & Milner, *Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery, and Psychiatry* (1957), PubMed Central - The original 1957 report describing the memory loss that followed H.M.'s operation. Reading even the first pages shows what a landmark clinical paper looks like.

6. [Postmortem examination of patient H.M.'s brain based on histological sectioning and digital 3D reconstruction](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms4122) - Annese et al., *Nature Communications* (2014) - The open-access study that sliced and digitally rebuilt H.M.'s brain. It shows which parts of his hippocampus were actually removed and which survived.

7. [18.1 Memory is Classified Based on Time Course and Type of Information Stored](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/18-1-memory-is-classified-based-on-time-course-and-type-of-information-stored) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - A free textbook section on short-term, working, and long-term memory, consolidation, and reconsolidation. It's a good next step after this chapter.

8. [18.4 Synaptic Mechanisms of Long-Term Memory](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/18-4-synaptic-mechanisms-of-long-term-memory) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Explains LTP, LTD, NMDA and AMPA receptors, and the Bliss and Lømo experiment in more depth, with helpful diagrams.

9. [The right time to learn: mechanisms and optimization of spaced learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5126970/) - Smolen, Zhang & Byrne, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* (2016), PubMed Central - A review of why spaced training beats massed training, from sea slugs to people. It connects study habits directly to cell biology.

10. [Epilepsy and Seizures](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/epilepsy-and-seizures) - National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) - The NIH overview of epilepsy, including the hippocampus's role in temporal lobe epilepsy and how modern epilepsy surgery works. It shows how medicine changed after H.M.
