# References: Vision

1. [6.2 The Retina](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/6-2-the-retina) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) - Explains the retina's cell layers, rods versus cones, phototransduction, and color blindness in clear steps. It is the main source for this chapter's photoreceptor numbers and cone sensitivity peaks.
2. [6.4 The Thalamus and Primary Visual Cortex](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/6-4-the-thalamus-and-primary-visual-cortex) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* - Covers the crossing at the optic chiasm, the layered LGN, and Hubel and Wiesel's discovery of edge-detecting cells in V1. Great for readers who want more detail on simple and complex cells.
3. [6.5 Extrastriate Cortex](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/6-5-extrastriate-cortex) - OpenStax, *Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience* - Describes the dorsal and ventral streams, the motion area MT, and face-selective neurons. A good next step after Section 5.10.
4. [Color Blindness](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness) - National Eye Institute (NIH) - A plain-language overview of color vision deficiency, who gets it, and how people adapt. Useful for checking facts about how common it is.
5. [Pituitary Tumors](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/pituitary-tumors) - National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NIH) - Explains what pituitary tumors are, their symptoms (including vision changes), and how they are diagnosed and treated. Good background for the Bedside case.
6. [Pituitary tumor](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000704.htm) - MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia (U.S. National Library of Medicine) - A short patient-friendly page on symptoms, tests such as visual field testing and MRI, and transsphenoidal surgery through the nose.
7. [Localizing the Lesion (Visual Fields)](https://novel.utah.edu/collection-materials/jonathan-trobe/fingertips/Visual_Fields/Localizing_The_Lesion.html) - Jonathan Trobe, NOVEL (Neuro-Ophthalmology Virtual Education Library), University of Utah - A neuro-ophthalmologist's guide matching each visual field pattern to a lesion site. Advanced, but excellent for Brain Bee competitors who want to check every pattern in the simulator.
8. [Optic radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_radiation) - Wikipedia - Describes the path of the optic radiation from the LGN to V1, including Meyer's loop and the "pie in the sky" defect. Use it as a starting point and check claims against the other sources here.
9. [One brain, two visual systems](https://www.bps.org.uk/psychologist/one-brain-two-visual-systems) - Mel Goodale and David Milner, *The Psychologist* (British Psychological Society), 2006 - The scientists behind the "perception versus action" idea tell the story of patient D.F. in their own words. A readable look at how one patient changed a field.
10. [Clinical efficacy of optical coherence tomography parameters to predict the visual field outcome following pituitary adenoma surgery](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0313521) - Han et al., *PLoS ONE*, 2024 - An open-access study showing that a thicker retinal nerve fiber layer before surgery is linked to better visual field recovery. Shows how a retina scan can help predict a patient's outcome.
